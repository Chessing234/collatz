# Device: the orbital ideal

Agent 6 — algebraic geometry / ideals.  Not a residue sieve, not a renamed
accumulator.

No Collatz claim is made.  In particular this file does not assert that a
never-dropping orbit is finite, nor that any of the chains below is forced to
be strictly nested forever.

---

## The hoped-for machine, and the polarity trap

The shape one wants is:

> to an orbit prefix `(n_0, …, n_k)` associate an ideal (or module, or lattice)
> `I(n_0, …, n_k)`, so that extending the prefix is inclusion of ideals, and
> a strictly nested chain in a Noetherian ring cannot be infinite.

That last clause is the wrong polarity.  Noetherian rings have **ACC**, not
DCC.  `ℤ`, `ℤ[x]`, and `ℚ[x]` are Noetherian and not Artinian:
`(2) ⊃ (4) ⊃ (8) ⊃ ⋯` and `(x) ⊃ (x(x−1)) ⊃ (x(x−1)(x−2)) ⊃ ⋯` are strictly
descending and infinite.  An infinite orbit of distinct points producing a
strictly descending chain of vanishing ideals in `ℚ[x]` is therefore not a
contradiction.  It would be a contradiction in an **Artinian** ring, but the
natural Artinian replacements (`𝔽_p[x]/(x^p−x)`, `ℤ/Nℤ`) are finite for
independent reasons, and the chain stabilises because the residue ring is
finite, not because of Collatz.

The usable polarity is the opposite one:

> extending the prefix **enlarges** the ideal (or shrinks a gcd).  Then ACC
> in a Noetherian ring, or ACC of submodules of `ℤ^2`, forces eventual
> stabilisation.  Stabilisation is a theorem about the object, not about
> Collatz.

What follows is an autopsy of the natural candidates, a negative theorem for
the difference ideal in `ℤ`, and a repaired object that is not secretly `(0)`
or `(1)`.

---

## Candidate autopsy

### A.  The excursion identity, as a generator

An excursion `IsExcursion n v u W e` is the identity

    3^v · u  =  2^W · e + 1.

The ideal in `ℤ` generated by `3^v u − 2^W e − 1` is `(0)`.  The same
vanishing in `ℤ[x, y]` for the graph equations `2y − (3x+1)` (odd) and
`2y − x` (even), evaluated on a realised prefix, is again `(0)`.  Too
trivial: the generator is the map, not a measurement of the prefix.

### B.  Vanishing ideals in `ℤ[x]`

`I_k = { f ∈ ℤ[x] : f(n_i) = 0 for all i ≤ k }`.  Extending the prefix
shrinks the ideal.  Over `ℚ[x]` one has `I_k = (∏_{i≤k} (x−n_i))` for distinct
points, strictly descending on a divergent orbit.  Harmless in a non-Artinian
ring, as above.  Eliminating the extra variables from the graph of `T` in
`ℤ[x_0, …, x_k]` returns the same content: the elimination ideal in `ℤ[x_0]`
is `(x_0 − n_0, d_k)` with `d_k` the gcd of pairwise differences, and that
is candidate C.

### C.  The difference ideal in `ℤ`  — negative theorem

Let `d_k(n)` be the gcd of `{ |T^{i}(n) − T^{j}(n)| : i, j ≤ k }`.  Equivalently
(because a telescoping sum of consecutive differences is a pairwise difference)
the gcd of the consecutive steps `|T^{i+1}(n) − T^i(n)|` for `i < k`.  Write
`I_k = (d_k) ⊆ ℤ`.  Extending the prefix adjoins more differences, so `d_{k+1}`
divides `d_k` and `I_k ⊆ I_{k+1}`.  This is an ascending chain of ideals in the
Noetherian ring `ℤ`, so it stabilises.

It stabilises at `(1)` as soon as the prefix contains **one odd step and one
even step**.  All prefix values are then congruent modulo `d_k`; the even step
forces `d_k` to divide the even value, hence (with the congruence) to divide
every value; the odd step is `2 T(m) = 3m+1`, so `d_k` divides both `m` and
`3m+1`, hence divides `1`.

That is `DeviceIdeal.mixed_steps_unit`.  Any mixed-parity accelerated prefix
of length at least two that actually takes both branches has difference ideal
`(1)`.  The construction is not identically `(1)` on length-one prefixes, but
it becomes `(1)` at the first mixed pair of steps, i.e. on every orbit that
the rest of the development cares about.  Secretly `(1)` for every interesting
prefix.  Discarded as a device.

(The odd-only variant — gcd of differences of Syracuse iterates — is divisible
by `4` on a never-dropper, all of whose odd points are `3 (mod 4)`, but is
again a restatement of residue information the sieve already has.)

### D.  Fitting ideals of the affine pieces at clock time

The `k`-step affine law is the matrix `[[3^{a}, C], [0, 2^k]]` with
`a = oddCount(n,k)`.  The `0`-th Fitting ideal of its cokernel is
`(det) = (3^a · 2^k)`, which does not form a chain as `k` grows.  The `1`-st
Fitting ideal is `(3^a, C, 2^k) = (1)` because `3^a` is odd.  Constant `(1)`.

The `2 × (k+1)` matrix with columns `(3^{oddCount n i}, 2^i)` for `i = 0, …, k`
has a `2`-minor already at `i=0,1` equal to `|2 − 3^{a_1}| ∈ {1,1}`.  So the
Fitting content is `1` after one step.  Again secretly `(1)`.  The clock-time
exponents `(a, L) = (a, i)` cannot repeat, because `i` is strictly increasing;
unique factorisation then forbids a vanishing minor.

### E.  The `ℤ`-module of relations among `{3^{v_i}, 2^{W_i}}` as elements of `ℤ`

The ideal they generate is `(gcd(3^{v_i}, 2^{W_i})) = (1)`.  The syzygy module
on those elements lives in a growing free module `ℤ^{k+1}`, so it is not a
chain in a fixed object.  The object that *is* a chain is the lattice in `ℤ^2`
they span.  That is the device.

## The object: Fitting content of the excursion lattice

To a list of excursion types `(v_i, W_i)` associate the `2 × n` integer matrix
with columns

    ( 3^{v_i} ,  2^{W_i} )^T  ∈  ℤ^2.

Let `Λ(ps)` be the subgroup of `ℤ^2` they generate, and let `typeFit(ps)` be
the gcd of all `2×2` minors

    | 3^{v}  2^{W'}  −  3^{v'}  2^{W} |.

This is the `0`-th Fitting ideal of `ℤ^2 / Λ(ps)`, evaluated as a principal
ideal of `ℤ` (a PID).  The `1`-st Fitting ideal is generated by all entries,
hence is `(1)` as soon as both a `3`-power and a positive `2`-power appear —
the wrong Fitting index, constantly `(1)`.  The `0`-th index is the content.

**Extension is inclusion.**  Appending types adjoins columns, so `Λ` grows,
the gcd of minors drops or stays, and `(typeFit(ps)) ⊆ (typeFit(ps ++ qs))`
in `ℤ`.  Equivalently `typeFit(ps ++ qs) ∣ typeFit(ps)`
(`DeviceIdeal.typeFit_append_dvd`).  ACC of ideals of `ℤ`, or ACC of
submodules of `ℤ^2`, forces eventual stabilisation of the lattice index.

**Vanishing is rigidity of type.**  Unique factorisation in `ℕ` for the primes
`2` and `3` (`DeviceIdeal.two_three_unique`) gives

    typeFit(ps) = 0   ⇔   all pairs `(v_i, W_i)` are equal

(`DeviceIdeal.typeFit_eq_zero_iff`).  In particular the leftover light class
of repeated `(v,W) = (2,1)` — consecutive light excursions, the residue
`n ≡ 11 (mod 16)` staying in `3 (mod 4)` — has **vanishing** Fitting content
at every finite prefix (`typeFit_replicate`).  The device is blind exactly
where the present leftover lives, and that is a theorem about the object, not
a failure of bookkeeping.

**It is not constant.**  Two distinct types produce a positive minor:

| types | `typeFit` |
|---|---|
| `(2,1), (2,1)` | `0` |
| `(2,1), (1,1)` | `12` |
| `(2,1), (2,2)` | `18` |
| `(2,1), (3,1)` | `36` |
| `(2,1), (4,3)` | `90` |
| `(3,1), (4,1)` | `108` |
| `(2,1), (3,1), (4,1)` | `36` |
| `(2,1), (3,1), (4,3)` | `18` |

The last row is the point of a gcd of several minors: a third type can drop
the index.  Kernel-checked in `DeviceIdeal` as `rfl`.

A type *change* along an orbit is exactly the event that moves the ideal off
`(0)`.  A never-dropper that stays in one excursion type forever has
`typeFit = 0` at every finite prefix; one that eventually uses two types has
a positive integer invariant which can only drop by divisibility thereafter.
That is a lattice measurement of the `(v,W)`-word.  It is not a residue class,
not an accumulator, and not a ranking.

## Repair, for the rigid-type blind spot: cofactor Fitting

The type matrix discards the odd cofactor `u` in `n + 1 = 2^v u`.  Put it
back.  Columns

    ( 3^{v_i} · u_i  ,  2^{W_i} )^T,

and `coFit` the gcd of those minors.  On a repeated type `(v,W)` this is

    coMinor((v,u,W), (v,u',W))  =  3^v · 2^W · |u − u'|

(`DeviceIdeal.coMinor_same_type`).  For the light class `(2,1)` that is
`18 |u − u'|`.  Odd cofactors differ by an even number, so a genuine change
of `u` produces a multiple of `36`, never `1`, and never `0` unless `u`
repeats (a one-excursion cycle, which `(2,1)` does not admit: `e = n` would
force `u = −1`).

So:

* `typeFit` sees *which excursion letters appear*;
* `coFit` sees *cofactor drift inside a single letter*.

The second is the object that still moves on a pure `(2,1)`-word.  Extending
the prefix again grows the ideal (`coFit_append_dvd`).  The stable value is
the gcd of all pairwise `|u_i − u_j|`, scaled by `3^v 2^W`.  That is the
difference ideal of the cofactor sequence, which candidate C killed for the
orbit values themselves by mixed parity; the cofactors of a rigid-type word
are all odd, and the scaled gcd need not be `1`.

## What this is not

It is not a proof that an infinite non-dropper is impossible.  Stabilisation
of `typeFit` says only that the set of excursion types used along a prefix
eventually generates a lattice of constant index in `ℤ^2`.  An infinite word
on a single letter is a legitimate infinite chain at index `0`.  Stabilisation
of `coFit` says the cofactors eventually lie in a fixed arithmetic
progression; it does not bound them.

It is not an S-unit equation.  The minors `3^v 2^{W'} − 3^{v'} 2^W` are the
`2`-minors of a lattice, not a closed cycle relation `Σ 3^{·} 2^{·} = 0`.
The S-unit identity `A + 3^a = C + 2^L` of `SUnitRelation` is an identity of
two sums along a *word*; this device is an identity of a *matrix of types*.

It is not a claim that the Fitting number drops infinitely often, nor that
its stable value contradicts never-dropping.  The device is the nested
object, the unique-factorisation vanishing criterion, and the cofactor
repair that remains non-constant on the leftover rigid word.
